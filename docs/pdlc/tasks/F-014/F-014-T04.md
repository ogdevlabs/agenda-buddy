The most sensitive data in the product: therapy and coaching notes about named individuals.
Threat T-201 is F-016's defect one layer along. NoteService.GetByAppointmentAsync TAKES a providerEmail, so the obvious route passes a client-supplied value through — handing any authenticated caller every provider's notes for any appointment identifier they can guess, and customers already receive those identifiers. The provider email comes from the `sub` claim, and NoteRequest deliberately has no providerEmail or appointmentIdentifier field at all: the cheapest guarantee that no later refactor starts trusting one.
Threat T-202: KeyNotFoundException and UnauthorizedAccessException both map to 403, indistinguishably. For a therapist the EXISTENCE of a note implies the session happened and was noteworthy.
The one relaxation, stated in the contract: an empty list and a 403 differ for a caller who has already proven they own the appointment. A non-owner gets 403 either way, so nothing leaks.
